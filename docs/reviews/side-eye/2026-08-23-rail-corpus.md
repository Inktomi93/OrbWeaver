## Verdict: SHIP WITH FIXES

The Corpus section is one of the better-built surfaces in this app. It is fully keyboard-operable
with a visible focus ring at **every one of 30 measured stops**, every canvas chart carries a real
`<table>` fallback, the search's degraded state ("Nothing matched strongly — these are the nearest,
at 34% or less") is the best copy I have read in this codebase, and the whole thing reflows to a
430px single column with **zero overflow**. Nothing is unreadable and nothing is unreachable.

What must not ship as-is: **the section's primary call-to-action is rendered twice, simultaneously,
40px apart** (detector-confirmed); **three different totals for the same library are visible in one
viewport** (12 / 12 / 10), including two of them three lines apart on the same rail; and **the same
job has two names and two homes** ("Run the understanding pass" here, "Distill it in the Refinery"
one click away).

**Design health: 23/40 — "mid" band.** (Calibration only. The band gates nothing; the finding list
is the deliverable.)

## Findings

### \[P1] The section's primary CTA is rendered twice, simultaneously, in one viewport

**What.** Opening the detail panel puts the *identical* "Read your library back to you." card in
CONTENT and in the CONTEXT panel's Archetypes tab at the same time — same `h2`, same 3-sentence
body, same "Turn on memory", same **primary** "Run the understanding pass", same "All jobs in
Settings → Jobs". Receipt, one eval on the live surface:

```
"14 groups; invitation-groups=2; runbuttons=2"
```

and the deterministic scanner found it independently, three times:

```
P3 duplicate-action-door … 2x button "run the understanding pass"
P3 duplicate-action-door … 2x button "all jobs in settings → jobs"
P3 duplicate-action-door … 2x button "turn on memory"
```

(`reports/design-audit/root.json`, route `/` + `--goto corpus` + detail panel open.)

**Why it hurts a user.** Two identical primary buttons in one view is the classic "which one is the
real one" hesitation, and it costs the CONTEXT panel its entire job: §14 says CONTEXT is *detail +
config OF the active artifact*, never a second copy of CONTENT. With nothing selected, the Archetypes
tab has nothing to be about, so it borrows CONTENT's hero. §13's IA single-homing rule and §14
physics 4 ("one `primary` action per region at rest") are both broken. Screenshot:
`reports/snaps/corpus-context-open.png`.

**Fix.** `distill: the Archetypes tab's pre-pass state — receipt: invitation-groups=1, runbuttons=1
with the detail panel open, plus before/after shots.` The Archetypes tab with no selection should
show the archetype *content* (the eight families, which today only live in CONTENT) or a one-line
pointer, never a second CTA. If the tab genuinely has nothing pre-pass, disable it and say why.

**Receipt.** `invitation-groups=2 runbuttons=2` (eval) · design-audit ×3 · `reports/snaps/corpus-context-open.png`.

### \[P1] Three different totals for the same library are visible at once — two of them on the same rail

**What.** One simultaneous read, single tick, one page load:

```
character.list totalCount        : 12
discovery.home coverage.characters: 12
visualArchetypes                 : 8 families, 10 members total
```

Which renders on screen as:

- `h1` — **"12 characters, grouped into eight visual families."**
- READINESS row 1 — `Visual families  8 families · 10 characters`
- READINESS row 2 — `Distilled — genre, tone, pitch  0 of 12`
- Visuals tab — `Portrait fit / Scored **10**`
- Map tab — *"**12 cards**, projected by semantic similarity"* (12 points, two of them named `Emily`)

Two characters are in no family, so the H1's sentence is arithmetically false — you can count the
members in the list beneath it (2+2+1+1+1+1+1+1 = 10) and get a different number from the headline.
And the READINESS rail states `10 characters` and `0 of 12` three lines apart.

**Why it hurts a user.** This is the section whose entire pitch is "read your library back to you."
The first sentence it says is wrong, and its own status rail contradicts itself. On the owner's real
library — where the image-embedding pass will always lag the character count — the gap is not 2, it
is hundreds, and the H1 becomes a confident lie about coverage. That is a trust defect, not a
rounding defect.

**Fix.** `clarify: the corpus H1 and the readiness rail — receipt: a single denominator across h1,
both rail rows, the Visuals "Scored" figure and the Map's card count, verified in one eval.` Say what
is actually true: *"10 of 12 characters grouped into eight visual families"* — the ungrouped
remainder is itself the useful fact (it tells you the embed pass has work left).

**Receipt.** one-tick eval (above) · `reports/snaps/corpus-now-tall.png` (h1 + rail in one frame) ·
`discovery.visualArchetypes` raw row `{"label":"male",…,"size":2,…}`.

**What.** With 12 characters in the library, the Corpus LIST pane renders

### \[P2] The "Invested, but quiet" shelf renders a bar chart in scrambled order

**What.** Measured bar widths, in DOM order, one eval:

```
Elias 166px · Birdie 130 · Hana 119 · Niko 72 · Kohaku 75 · Charlotte — · JFC — ·
Morgatha 284 · Calamity 202 · Sabine 180
```

(all bars `oklab(0.7 -0.0616 -0.0788 / 0.55)`, track `oklch(0.99 0.005 60 / 0.12)`, 284px max.)

The bars are correct — they are proportional to `tokensOut`, and the shelf's gloss says so ("Bars
compare tokens returned"). The *order* is also correct: the verb ranks
`messageCount × how-long-quiet` (`packages/server/src/domain/discovery/verbs/economics-insights.ts:66-92`,
`gemRank`). The problem is that those are two different quantities, so the shelf renders as a bar
chart whose bars go 166, 130, 119, 72, **75**, —, —, **284**, 202, 180. The longest bar is eighth.

**Why it hurts a user.** Every reader parses a stack of left-aligned proportional bars as a ranking.
This one isn't, and the disagreement is loud — the maximum sits two thirds of the way down, and one
adjacent pair (Niko 72 / Kohaku 75) inverts. The gloss discloses it in words; the picture overrides
the words. §13: the eye reads the chart before it reads the caption.

**Fix.** `layout: the gem shelf — receipt: monotonic bar widths in DOM order, or a visible rank
ordinal per tile.` Either bar the quantity you rank by, sort by the quantity you bar, or number the
tiles 1–10 so the reader knows the sequence is the datum and the bar is a second, independent one.

**Receipt.** bar-width eval (above) · `reports/snaps/corpus-now-tall.png`.

### \[P2] The Similarity tab shows a 93% pair you cannot act on, next to a rail that says the pass never ran

pre-filled with both sides, and a readiness-rail state that the disclaimer can honestly point at.`

**Receipt.** `--aria [aria-label="Corpus details"]` on the Similarity tab · clickable-ancestor eval

### \[P2] The eight "visual families" are labelled on four unrelated axes

**What.** The raw archetype rows carry four facets each — the sample row is
`{"label":"male","genre":null,"tone":null,"artStyle":"anime","palette":"cool","mood":"neutral","size":2}` —
but the rendered label picks ONE facet, and a different one per family. The rendered set is:

> **Male** · **Full-body** · **Close-up** · **Cool** · **Dark** · **Armor** · **Warm** · **Serene**

That is subject (Male), framing (Full-body, Close-up), colour temperature (Cool, Warm), value
(Dark), wardrobe (Armor) and mood (Serene), presented as one comparable list under the heading
"THE SHAPE OF YOUR LIBRARY".

**Why it hurts a user.** "Male" and "Close-up" are not alternatives to each other, so the list does
not partition anything — a character can obviously be all of male, close-up, cool and dark at once,
and the reader cannot tell why each one landed where it did. This is *not* a small-corpus artifact:
the label-selection rule is the same at 330 characters, and there it will produce eight equally
incomparable buckets over a much larger population.

**Fix.** `clarify: the visual-family label — receipt: eight labels drawn from one declared axis, or
a compound label naming the axes it combined ("anime · cool · full-body").` If the clustering is
genuinely multi-facet, say so in the label; if it is single-facet, pick the facet once.

**Receipt.** `discovery.visualArchetypes` raw row (above) · `reports/snaps/corpus-now-tall.png`.

### \[P2] Every query returns "12 results" — the count is the library size, not a match count

**What.** Query `dark sorceress` → status `12 results in Characters`, scores 60% / 42% / 41% / 41% /
38% / 37% / 37% / 33% / 31% / 31% / … Query `zzzqqqxx nonexistent` → status
`12 nearest results in Characters — nothing matched strongly`, scores 34% / 34% / 31% / 30% / …

The weak state is handled beautifully — a visible paragraph, "Nothing matched strongly — these are
the nearest, at 34% or less." The **strong** state gets no such framing: it reports 12 results, which
is simply the whole library, ranked.

**Why it hurts a user.** "12 results" for a two-word query reads as twelve matches. It is not; the
twelfth is a 31% cosine neighbour. And the count is the *same number* as the H1's "12 characters" ten
pixels to the right, which makes it read as a coincidence rather than a coincidence. (Note this is
a *presentation* finding about the search UI, not a claim about the retrieval boundary — I have not
judged this surface as retrieval.)

**Fix.** `clarify: the search result status — receipt: a visible count that names its own threshold,
in both the strong and weak states.` Either cut the tail below a stated floor, or label it — "3
strong matches · 9 nearest" — and render the count on screen, not only in the live region (today the
sighted user sees no count at all in the HIT state; the SR user does).

**Receipt.** `--aria [aria-label="Corpus list"]` in both states · `reports/snaps/search-hit.png`.

### \[P2] "Portrait fit" is three big numbers with no scale, no target and no definition

**What.** The Visuals tab leads with `Scored 10 · Mean fit 37% · Median fit 38%`, then
"WORST-MATCHED ART" listing every character at 29%–45%. The best portrait in the library scores 45%.
Nothing on the surface says what fit measures, what good looks like, or what to do about a low score.
Each row's supporting line is `anime · safe` — two more unexplained tokens.

**Why it hurts a user.** A metric where the entire population sits between 29% and 45% reads as
"everything is broken" to anyone who does not know the scale is not 0–100-as-quality. And the
heading "Worst-matched art" over a list containing *every* character implies the whole library is
mismatched. Nielsen #2 and #10.

**Fix.** `clarify: the Portrait fit block — receipt: a one-line definition adjacent to the figure,
plus a stated band (what counts as low), plus a gloss on "safe".` If 37% is a normal cosine
similarity between a card's text and its portrait, say that — the number is fine, the framing isn't.

**Receipt.** `--aria` on the Visuals tab · `reports/snaps/tab-visuals.png`.

## ARIA-navigability recommendations

The keyboard story here is **good** — 30 measured Tab stops, `fv=true` (`:focus-visible`) at every
single one, a correct roving tablist (one tab stop for five tabs), and canvas charts that carry real
`<table>` fallbacks. These are the specific fixes:

1. **The search combobox claims `aria-expanded="true"` with an empty popup, on a pristine load.**
   Receipt, no clicks, `--goto corpus --idle`:
   ```json
   {"expanded":"true","controls":"_r_4l_","active":true,
    "liveRegions":["status:\"App loaded.\"","status:\"0 suggestions\"","region:\"\""]}
   ```
   **Fix:** set `aria-expanded="false"` until the listbox actually has options, and do not render the
   suggestions live region until there is a query. Today a screen-reader user entering Corpus hears
   "combobox, expanded, 0 suggestions" before they hear what the section is.

2. **Two live regions fight during search.** `status: "0 suggestions"` persists alongside
   `status: "12 results in Characters"`, so every keystroke announces both. **Fix:** one `role="status"`
   for the result count; drop the suggestions region entirely when the combobox has no popup.

3. **Match scores and fit percentages sit OUTSIDE their row's control.** Search:
   `button "Morgatha, the Undying Dark": No pitch distilled` then a sibling `paragraph: 60%`.
   Visuals: `button "Hana Mizushima": anime · safe` then `text: 29%`. A screen-reader user tabbing the
   list never hears the number the list is sorted by. **Fix:** fold the value into the control's
   accessible name (`aria-label="Morgatha, the Undying Dark — 60% match"`), or move the `<p>` inside
   the button. The bar decoration should stay `aria-hidden` (it already is, correctly).

4. **The READINESS rail is one flat text node.** ARIA renders it as
   `text: "Visual families 8 families · 10 characters Distilled — genre, tone, pitch 0 of 10 Story
   themes not run Keywords not run Near-duplicates not run"` — no list, no label/value association,
   and the ✓ / ○ status glyphs are not announced at all. **Fix:** `<dl>` or `role="list"` with one
   `listitem` per pass, each carrying `<name> — <state>` as one accessible unit, and give the glyph an
   `aria-label` ("complete" / "not run") or drop it as decorative since the value column already
   carries the state in words.

5. **The visual-family rows are non-interactive text inside card-shaped containers.** ARIA shows the
   whole "The shape of your library" region as a single `text:` node — eight rows that *look* like
   list rows, with avatar stacks and hover-ready geometry, expose no list structure and no controls.
   **Fix:** `role="list"` + `listitem` per family at minimum; if a family is meant to be openable
   (it reads as though it should be), make each row a button naming its members.

6. **The Archetypes tabpanel is itself a focus stop** (`DIV[tabpanel] … fv=true | 367x693`), drawing a
   focus ring around the whole 367×693 panel. APG puts `tabindex="0"` on a tabpanel only when it
   contains no focusable children; this one contains three buttons. **Fix:** drop `tabindex="0"` from
   the panel.

7. **One skip link, and it only reaches CONTENT.** Same as the Chats sweep — there is no skip to the
   LIST pane, which on this section is where the search lives. **Fix (section-independent):** add a
   second skip target for the list region.

## Taste & flow verdict (blunt)

**Does it look like shit?** No. It looks *considered* — this is one of the few surfaces in the app
with a real editorial voice, and it earns it. The kicker/H1 pair is confident, the invitation card
reads like a person wrote it, and the search-target hint lines are the best micro-copy in the
codebase. The typography holds at every arm I measured; the Light theme renders correctly
(`bodyBg oklch(0.98 0.004 75)`, h1 15.56:1, body 14.43:1 — computed, not eyeballed).

What is wrong with it visually is **balance, not craft**. At the shipped desktop default the page is
three stacked sections at three different widths with a right column that dies a fifth of the way
down and leaves ~1,300px of black beside 1,700px of content. The eye keeps finding a column edge and
then losing it. The list-hidden arm is markedly better composed, which tells you the two-column model
was designed for the wide case and never re-checked at the default.

**Does it flow weird?** Yes, in one specific way: **the section's job is stated four times before you
can do it.** You land on a headline that tells you what it is, a card that tells you again at length,
a readiness rail that tells you again as a status list, and — if you open the detail panel — the same
card a second time. That is four surfaces competing to be the onboarding, and the actual decision
point (press one button) has three competing controls on it: "Turn on memory" (right-aligned at the
end of small print, where it reads as a footnote but is a state change), the primary CTA, and a link
out to Settings → Jobs. Working memory at the one decision point that matters: 3 actions + a
5-row status rail + a "we can't do themes yet" caveat = over the ≤4 line (§8).

**Is it intuitive cold?** Partly. A first-timer will understand "press the orange button". They will
*not* understand: why the left pane says their library is empty while the right shows twelve
characters; why "Male" and "Close-up" are the same kind of category; what 37% fit means; why a 93%
duplicate pair sits above a line saying no duplicates were found. Every one of those is a
five-second-test failure.

**More than one home for a concept** (§13 IA), the full list I found:

- the understanding-pass invitation — CONTENT **and** the Archetypes tab, at once (P1)
- the distil job — "Run the understanding pass" here, "Go to Refinery" in the dossier (P1)
- the library count — h1, two readiness rows, the Visuals "Scored" figure, the Map's card count (P1)
- character similarity — "Nearest pairs" and "Duplicate characters" on one tab, disagreeing, plus
  Compare on another tab that cannot be reached from either (P2)

## What's genuinely working — do not touch

1. **The weak-search state.** `"12 nearest results in Characters — nothing matched strongly"` plus the
   visible paragraph *"Nothing matched strongly — these are the nearest, at 34% or less."* A semantic
   search always returns everything; this is the app being honest about it instead of faking a
   zero-results screen. Model behaviour — copy this pattern to the Map tab's pre-pass state.
2. **The five search targets and their hint lines.** *"Search your images by likeness and caption —
   type what an image shows. A hit opens the card wearing it."* Each of the five explains its own
   scope in one sentence, including what happens when you click a hit. Do not shorten these.
3. **Canvas accessibility.** Both the Map and the Visual-facets chart ship a full `<table>` fallback
   (`row "Charlotte Other"`, `row "Anime 9"`) plus a keyed legend list. This is the correct answer to
   "canvas is invisible to every DOM instrument" and almost nobody does it.
4. **Keyboard operability.** 30 stops, `:focus-visible` true at every one, correct roving tablist,
   `role="listitem"` on the layout wrapper rather than the Button (the gem-tile source calls this out
   explicitly). Nothing to fix here except the six items above.
5. **The mobile CONTENT layout.** Single column, families stacked, readiness reflowed, gem tiles
   full-width, `no-overflow main: PASS overflow=0x0` at 430px. Only the *default pane* is wrong.

## The single biggest opportunity

**Make the CONTEXT panel earn its 384px.** Three of this review's P1s converge on it: it duplicates
CONTENT's hero because it has nothing of its own to show pre-pass; it holds the Similarity findings
that cannot reach the Compare tab six pixels away; and it is where the Map lives, disconnected from
the families list in CONTENT that is the same data by another projection. If the panel became what
§14 says CONTEXT is — *detail of the active artifact* — then selecting a character would drive it
(dossier facets, that character's family, its nearest neighbours, its portrait fit, a one-click
compare against any of them), the duplicate card disappears, the Similarity pair gets a destination,
and the LIST→CONTENT→CONTEXT chain finally works the way the rest of the shell does. That single
change retires findings 1, 6, and most of the flow verdict.

## Retractions — findings I formed and killed

1. **"The gem-tile and forgotten-gem rows have run-together accessible names."** I read
   `button "Elias Thorn5,964 tokens returned · 10 exchangeslast opened 2w ago"` off `--map` and
   started writing an accname finding. `--aria` on the same surface reports
   `button "Elias Thorn 5,964 tokens returned · 10 exchanges last opened 2w ago"` — properly spaced.
   `--map` prints `textContent`, which loses inter-element whitespace; the *accessible name* comes
   from `--aria`. **Killed.** (This is the documented map-vs-aria trap; I paid it anyway.)

2. **"The invitation card uses a `border-accent-on-rounded` slop tell."** I read an amber ring off the
   default-arm screenshot. Computed style at that arm:
   `border: 1px oklch(0.99 0.005 60 / 0.08)` — a neutral 8%-white hairline. It reads warm because it
   sits on a warm-hued dark card (`oklch(0.205 0.006 60)`). **Killed.** My eye is not a colorimeter.
   *(Note, not a finding: under `--appearance-preset maximal` the same border resolves to
   `oklab(0.770357 0.0848753 0.109179 / 0.28)` — genuinely accent-tinted — atop the sanctioned
   4-layer `--shadow-overlay` recipe. That is a global `data-elevation=glow` card treatment, not a
   corpus decision, so it is out of this review's scope; flagging it here so the next reviewer does
   not re-derive it.)*

3. **"Focus order in the gem shelf jumps rows out of visual order."** The walk showed y-coordinates
   376, 376, 490, 490, 605, 605, 704, 704, then **508, 508**. That looked like a DOM-order defect. It
   is the scroll container scrolling the focused tile into view between stops — coordinates are
   viewport-relative. **Killed**, no defect.

4. **"Mobile Corpus is a dead end — the content is unreachable."** The 430px default pane shows only
   the empty list, and I could see no labelled way out. `--map` on mobile shows
   `button "Show Corpus overview"` and `button "Show details"` — both correctly named, both working
   (`corpus-mobile-content.png`). **Killed as a P1 dead end**; it survives as the P2 "mobile opens on
   the wrong pane".

5. **Inherited from the brief: "all 10 analysis passes Succeeded; every corpus surface should be
   populated; an empty tile is a FINDING."** `workloads.list` returns one row
   (`refresh-model-catalog`, 01:27:47) and `server.log:6401` records the database being dropped at
   01:27:46. **The premise is void** — every empty surface on this sweep has one cause, and it is not
   the corpus UI. I have filed no finding on the basis of an empty tile.

6. **Instrument false positives I did not forward.** `design-audit` reported 12 `nested-card` P3s.
   Eleven of them are `[data-slot=avatar-stack-item]` inside a family row's `card-root` — i.e.
   **avatars**, which carry border+radius+background by construction. The twelfth,
   `[data-slot=autocomplete-input-group]`, is an interactive island, which CD1 explicitly sanctions.
   Only `[data-slot=archetypes-not-run] > [data-slot=card-root]` is arguably real, and it is the
   duplicated invitation card already filed as P1. **11/12 false; avatar-stack items are a
   nested-card FP class the walker does not yet exclude** — worth a tooling row under the
   fix-tools-as-we-find-them-lying rule, at the same priority as any other lying-instrument finding.
